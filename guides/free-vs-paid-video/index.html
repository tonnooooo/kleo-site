Free animatic or paid AI film: choose by deliverable

The meaningful comparison is the output you need. Kleo's starter offer supports an animatic; its paid film adds generated moving footage. Compare those deliverables and the current quote instead of comparing the word free with a price in isolation.

Compare the output, not just the price

An animatic and a film answer different production questions. An animatic helps you judge the planned sequence and narration. A film adds generated moving footage that must be reviewed in its own right. Paying for that footage does not guarantee the story, facts or visual continuity will be correct.

Use the same brief when comparing

Suppose your story follows a lighthouse keeper noticing a signal, searching the shore and answering it. Keep that sequence, the language, the aspect ratio and the intended ending fixed. In an animatic, you can judge whether the clue and response are understandable. In a film, you also inspect whether the keeper’s action, the light and the shoreline move plausibly.

If you change the story, duration and look between the two jobs, a better result does not tell you which change helped. Keep a short comparison record: approved brief, product, quote, actual result, observations and the next decision. This is an editorial comparison method, not a claim that this lighthouse story has been rendered.

  1. Write what the deliverable must contain: the story outcome, narration language, framing and whether generated motion is essential.
  2. Ask for each product’s current quote separately. Specify which one you are approving, rather than saying “make the video” after discussing both.
  3. Compare the actual downloaded result with the same approved brief. Keep changes to the story separate from defects in the pictures or audio.

Budget two jobs if you want both

A 30-second animatic costs 10 credits and a 30-second film costs 20, so the two planned renders total 30 credits. This is a production-cost sum, not proof that all credits in an account can pay for either product. Film eligibility and the usable balance must be checked separately.

The animatic fee is not described as a deposit automatically deducted from a later film. Do not promise a conversion, discount or shared render unless the actual quote states it. Check sound, captions and graphic options before approving the treatment, and read the Terms for the Kleo mark and uses that require permission.

A practical method

  1. Write what the finished result must contain.
  2. Decide whether a story preview is sufficient or moving footage is essential.
  3. Include additional editing and any extra render in your own project estimate.

An example brief to adapt

Written illustration of this method; not a report of a rendered film or a customer result.

Help me compare an animatic and a film for a 30-second fictional story: a lighthouse keeper notices a signal, searches the shore and answers it. Keep the English narration, realistic look and 16:9 format consistent. Compare the pictures I would receive, the questions each result can answer and each job’s exact quote. Check film eligibility and usable credits separately from the total balance. Do not buy credits or render either version; let me choose one first.

Review before moving on

  • Is the comparison based on output?
  • Are additional tasks included?
  • Is the selected option appropriate for the intended use?

Record a concrete observation beside each check: a line in the treatment, a source passage or a timestamp in the downloaded film. If you cannot inspect an item yet, leave it open. A request in a brief is an intention; the result is what must be reviewed.

What to avoid

A cheaper preview can be useful even when it is not the final deliverable. Conversely, paying for footage will not automatically fix an unclear brief, unsupported claim or inconsistent story.
