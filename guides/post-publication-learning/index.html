Learn from published videos without guessing causes

Performance review should separate observations from explanations. A title change, a different topic and a new publishing time can occur together, so a higher view count alone does not identify what caused the difference.

A practical method

  1. Record the actual title, topic, format and publication context.
  2. Compare similar videos before attributing a result to one word.
  3. Choose a focused change for the next test and document the hypothesis.

An example brief to adapt

Written illustration of this method; not a report of a rendered film or a customer result.

Help me review my channel observations. Several titles containing free received more views. List alternative explanations, including topic and audience differences, and propose a fair next comparison. Do not invent search volumes or claim that the word alone caused the result.

Review before moving on

  • Are comparisons reasonably similar?
  • Are claims based on observed data?
  • Is the next experiment specific?

Record a concrete observation beside each check: a line in the treatment, a source passage or a timestamp in the downloaded film. If you cannot inspect an item yet, leave it open. A request in a brief is an intention; the result is what must be reviewed.

What to avoid

Avoid treating a few successful examples as a universal rule. Use the pattern to guide a test while keeping the promise to viewers accurate.
