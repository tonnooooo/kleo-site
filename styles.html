THE WORKFLOW

How a film is built

The two looks share one pipeline. The look is settled at step 0, in the treatment, and everything after it is generated under that decision: the storyboard, every shot, the narration, the cut and the 4K 60 fps render.

  1. Treatment. Your assistant hands the request to kleo_adapt_prompt, which reads it against the intake: subject, length, format and look, plus audience, tone and what must appear as options, and asks for whatever is missing instead of assuming it. Back comes the treatment: logline, angle, opening, acts, ending, look, pacing, narrator. The look is decided here, before a frame exists.
  2. Storyboard. kleo_storyboard_guide sets out how the storyboard is written under that treatment: a direction block, the scenes, the shots and the limits.
  3. Footage. kleo_create_video checks everything, states the exact credits and starts the render on a GPU rented for that job; nothing is charged until it accepts. Every shot is then generated as moving footage from its own frame, in the look the treatment chose.
  4. Narration. One narrator voices the film, in English or Italian. No background music, no captions.
  5. Assembly. The shots and the narration are cut together into one film. Nothing goes on the frame unless the film’s treatment asks for it.
  6. Render. An MP4 at 4K 60 fps, 3840×2160 for 16:9 or 2160×3840 for 9:16, typically 25–35 minutes on the rented GPU. Your assistant follows the progress and kleo_get_result hands it the link, good for 7 days.

Whatever the look, the standard film carries one narrator and nothing else: no background music, no captions, no on-screen text. Dates, diagrams or subtitles appear only when a film’s treatment asks for them, as the treatment of Signal Delay did.
