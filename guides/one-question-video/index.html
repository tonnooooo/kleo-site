Build a video around one answer

A useful explainer begins with a question that can be answered within the running time. Narrow the question before choosing a visual style: a polished film about everything usually leaves the viewer with nothing specific.

A practical method

  1. Write the viewer's question in their own words, without a product slogan.
  2. Draft a one-sentence answer; remove any branch that does not support it.
  3. Choose three visible moments that make the answer understandable without adding a second topic.

An example brief to adapt

Written illustration of this method; not a report of a rendered film or a customer result.

Plan a 45-second narrated film about why a bicycle needs gears, for someone learning to ride. Explain the practical trade-off, not the history of bicycles. Show a flat road, a hill and a rider changing effort. Use animation and English narration. Return a treatment before rendering.

Review before moving on

  • Can a viewer repeat the answer in one sentence?
  • Does each scene support that answer?
  • Have unsupported technical claims been removed?

Record a concrete observation beside each check: a line in the treatment, a source passage or a timestamp in the downloaded film. If you cannot inspect an item yet, leave it open. A request in a brief is an intention; the result is what must be reviewed.

What to avoid

Avoid promising to explain an entire subject in a Short. If the outline contains five unrelated questions, split the brief before any production credits are spent.
