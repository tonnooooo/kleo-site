Prepare a generated film for YouTube upload

Publishing on YouTube includes reviewing the final file and completing the platform's upload settings. Kleo's public workflow provides a downloadable result; it does not publish to your channel automatically.

Plan a film for YouTube Shorts

YouTube's current guidance categorizes eligible square or vertical uploads up to three minutes as Shorts. Kleo offers a vertical 9:16 option; choose the duration and framing deliberately, then check current YouTube requirements at upload time.

Write a video title that matches the content

A useful title identifies the actual subject and gives a truthful reason to watch. Curiosity can help, but the film should deliver what the title promises rather than rely on an exaggerated result or an absent feature.

Write a useful video description with sources

A video description can help viewers find the original evidence, credits and next step. Put the main context first, then organize source links so readers can connect them with the claims in the film.

Prepare captions for a narrated film

Captions need to reflect the actual audio and remain synchronized with it. Plan a reviewed captioning step in your publishing workflow instead of assuming a generated video automatically supplies accurate, ready-to-use captions.

Brief a thumbnail without misrepresenting the film

A thumbnail should communicate the film's actual subject through a clear image. Use a genuine frame or an honest illustrative design; avoid depicting a result, interface or person the video does not substantiate.

Review AI disclosure before uploading

YouTube provides a disclosure process for certain meaningfully altered or synthetic content. Whether a particular film requires disclosure depends on its content and the current policy; review the official guidance rather than treating all AI use as identical.

Maintain sources after a video is published

Published videos can outlive the pages they cite. Keep a record of what supported the film and check important links when updating descriptions or responding to questions.

Repurpose a film for another aspect ratio

Changing aspect ratio can remove story information from the frame. Decide whether a crop, an edited layout or a newly planned version best preserves the action; do not assume a landscape film will become a clear Short automatically.

Help viewers navigate a video series

Series navigation helps a viewer understand where to start and what to watch next. Use clear episode titles and descriptions that explain each video's distinct question rather than relying only on numbered labels.

Learn from published videos without guessing causes

Performance review should separate observations from explanations. A title change, a different topic and a new publishing time can occur together, so a higher view count alone does not identify what caused the difference.
