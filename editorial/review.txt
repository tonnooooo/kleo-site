factual-review|Fact-check a narrated video before release|Factual review compares the final spoken and visual claims with their evidence. An approved script is only the starting point: generated visuals or later edits can introduce implications that were never checked.|List factual statements in the final narration.~Compare each one with the approved source material.~Watch for visual claims, including invented charts, uniforms or event details.|Build a claim-review sheet from my narration. Use columns for the statement, supporting source, qualification and approval status. Flag unsupported claims instead of supplying plausible replacements. I will separately check the rendered pictures for misleading implications.|Does the final wording match the source?~Are dates and units correct?~Do visuals imply unverified facts?|A citation at the end does not validate every scene. Check each consequential claim, especially when the film concerns real people, history or technical matters.
visual-artifact-review|Inspect generated footage for visual artifacts|Visual review looks for distortions that distract from the story or change its meaning. Check the whole film at normal speed, then inspect suspicious moments rather than treating a single attractive frame as approval.|Watch for changing anatomy, object shape and background lettering.~Compare adjacent shots where identity or location matters.~Classify issues by their impact on comprehension before requesting revisions.|Create a review list for a fictional workshop film. Prioritize hands interacting with objects, changing tool shapes and the continuity of the central clock. Separate minor background imperfections from errors that make the repair story impossible to understand.|Does the artifact alter the story?~Is it visible at normal viewing size?~Does it recur across several shots?|Do not assume every imperfection needs a full new render. Identify the problem first, then decide whether editing, a different shot or a revised concept is appropriate.
mobile-review|Review a film on a phone|A mobile review tests whether the intended message survives smaller viewing conditions. Important action, captions and added text may be readable on a large monitor but unclear in the actual feed.|Watch the final export at a realistic phone size.~Check the first scene and closing action without pausing.~Preview platform overlays around important visual information.|Make a mobile-review checklist for a 9:16 fictional book-exchange film. Focus on the recognizable book, the narrator's invitation and any captions added later. Do not use desktop readability as the final acceptance criterion.|Can the subject be identified quickly?~Is text readable without zooming?~Do interface overlays hide essential details?|A vertical aspect ratio alone does not make a video mobile-friendly. Scene complexity, contrast and the position of added text still need review on the intended platform.
silent-review|Use a silent pass to test visual clarity|Watching without audio reveals whether the sequence communicates its basic actions and whether important context depends entirely on narration. It is a diagnostic pass, not a requirement that every narrated film tell its complete argument silently.|Watch once without consulting the script.~Write down moments where identity, place or action is unclear.~Decide whether the fix belongs in framing, editing, captions or narration.|Review this storyboard as if the audio were muted. Tell me where a viewer would lose track of the parcel and why the courier changes direction. Do not invent new plot points. Suggest the smallest visual clarification for each confusion.|Can the main action be followed?~Are transitions understandable?~Is essential audio available through reviewed captions?|Do not remove useful narration merely to pass a silent test. The aim is to discover avoidable ambiguity and plan access to the spoken information.
brief-compliance|Check a rendered film against its approved brief|A brief-compliance review compares the actual deliverable with explicit requirements. Separate factual or contractual requirements from subjective preferences so that approval does not become an endless search for a different mood.|Copy the approved requirements into a checklist.~Record the observed result and timestamp for each item.~Resolve critical mismatches before optional polish.|Turn the approved brief into an acceptance sheet. Include the requested language, aspect ratio, ending and essential prop. Leave a place for timestamps and observations from the rendered file. Do not mark a requirement passed just because it appeared in the prompt.|Are requirements observable?~Are failures tied to timestamps?~Are must-haves separated from preferences?|A tool reporting completion means the job finished, not that every creative requirement was fulfilled. Human review remains part of delivery.
source-context-review|Check that a source has not lost its context|A film can quote an accurate statement while presenting it in a misleading context. Review the source's scope, date, uncertainty and intended population before turning a narrow finding into a broad conclusion.|Read the source around the cited passage.~Check whether the statement applies to the situation shown.~Preserve meaningful uncertainty in narration and publication notes.|Review my proposed narration against the supplied source excerpt and surrounding context. Flag any sentence that turns a possibility into a certainty or a local finding into a universal rule. Suggest narrower wording for approval without adding new facts.|Does the scope match?~Has uncertainty survived the edit?~Is the source current enough for the claim?|Shortening a script can remove precisely the qualification that made the original statement accurate. Treat those edits as factual decisions, not merely stylistic ones.
reconstruction-labels|Label illustrative reconstructions clearly|A generated reconstruction should not be presented as a recording of the event it illustrates. Decide how viewers will understand the status of the images through the narration, visible labels added in editing and the publication context.|Identify scenes depicting real events that were not actually filmed for this project.~Choose clear wording that describes the images as illustrative.~Review the destination platform's current disclosure requirements.|Plan an illustrative sequence accompanying a sourced historical account. Do not imply that the generated scenes are archival recordings. Add a production note identifying which scenes need an illustrative-reconstruction label in the final edit, and retain a source list for the factual narration.|Could a viewer mistake the scene for evidence?~Is the label clear where it matters?~Does the description explain the production method?|A realistic appearance can strengthen an unintended claim of authenticity. Do not rely solely on a vague disclaimer placed far from the material it explains.
asset-record|Keep an asset and permission record|An asset record connects each externally supplied image, audio file or quotation with its origin and permitted use. It helps the publishing team avoid treating available material as automatically cleared for a new project.|List every external asset and its original source.~Save the applicable permission or license information.~Identify unresolved uses before the final edit is approved.|Create an asset record for the files I plan to add after downloading the film. Include the file name, creator, source, usage terms, required credit and verification status. Leave unknown fields unresolved rather than inferring ownership.|Is the original source recorded?~Are required credits included?~Are uncertain permissions resolved by the owner?|This production record is not a legal clearance opinion. When a use is uncertain or consequential, obtain the appropriate permission or qualified advice rather than guessing from a search result.
review-rounds|Organize review rounds without conflicting edits|Separate review rounds by purpose: first the story and facts, then the render, then the publication package. A single unstructured comment thread can mix essential corrections with incompatible personal preferences.|Assign one person to consolidate feedback.~Ask reviewers to identify a timestamp or treatment line.~Close each round with a written decision and a version label.|Organize feedback on this film into factual corrections, story clarity, visual defects and optional preferences. Combine duplicates and highlight contradictions for a decision. Do not implement mutually incompatible notes or commission another render until priorities are approved.|Is feedback consolidated?~Are contradictions resolved?~Does everyone review the same version?|More reviewers do not automatically improve the result. Give each person a clear remit and preserve a single approved version before moving to the next stage.
export-inspection|Inspect the actual exported video file|The final file should be checked for playback, duration, framing, sound and completeness. Treat advertised output specifications as expectations to verify, not as proof of the properties of a particular downloaded MP4.|Open the full downloaded file in a media player.~Inspect its resolution, frame rate and duration using file information.~Watch the beginning and end, then the entire film with audio.|Prepare an export checklist for my downloaded Kleo MP4. Record the actual dimensions, frame rate and duration from the file. Check for missing audio, frozen sections and an abruptly cut ending. Do not fill these fields from the marketing page.|Does the file play to the end?~Do actual properties meet the brief?~Is the edited export distinct from the original render?|A preview on a webpage may use a different rendition from the downloadable master. Inspect the deliverable you will actually publish or send to the client.
release-signoff|Approve the whole publication package|Release approval should cover the film, title, description, captions, thumbnail and destination. Changes made after the video review can introduce new claims or reveal information that was not approved.|Collect the final versions in one review package.~Confirm factual wording and asset credits across all items.~Record who approved release and which version they approved.|Prepare a release checklist for this video and its upload materials. Include the actual final file, reviewed captions, title, description, source links and disclosure choice. Mark unresolved items clearly; do not treat video approval as permission to publish different wording.|Are all materials the final versions?~Are claims consistent across them?~Is publication explicitly approved?|A carefully reviewed video can still be misrepresented by an exaggerated title or thumbnail. Evaluate the package as viewers will encounter it.
