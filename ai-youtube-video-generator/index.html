Build a film around one clear idea.

Start with the question your viewer wants answered. Give Kleo the facts, the audience and the point you want the viewer to remember. An explainer needs a clear sequence; a story needs a setting, a change and an ending.

For a landscape channel video, choose 16:9. For a Short, choose 9:16. A film runs from 15 seconds to 5 minutes, with English or Italian narration by one narrator. Every shot is generated as moving footage from its own frame, so a 3-minute explainer is a sequence of scenes that move, not a slideshow of stills.

What Kleo asks before it plans.

Kleo reads your request against a short intake. Whatever the request does not say, the assistant asks in one message and waits for your answers. Nothing is assumed: Kleo never picks a subject, a length, a format or a look for you.

  • SubjectWhat the film is about, and the point the viewer should remember.
  • LengthFrom 15 seconds to 5 minutes.
  • Format16:9 for a YouTube video, 9:16 for a Short.
  • LookRealistic or animation.
  • OptionalAudience, tone, and anything that must appear in the film.

A brief that answers all of it up front, like the ones below, skips the questions and goes straight to the treatment.

A production workflow inside your conversation.

01 · Write the brief

Describe your subject, length, format and look, and share the facts and tone you want the film to keep. You are talking to your own assistant; Kleo is the studio behind it.

02 · Answer the intake

Anything the brief leaves out, the assistant asks for in one message. Audience, tone and what must appear are optional; subject, length, format and look are not.

03 · Change the treatment

Before anything is rendered, the assistant shows you the treatment: the logline, the angle, the opening, the acts, the ending, the look, the pacing and the narrator. Change what you want; the film follows it.

04 · The storyboard

Under that treatment the assistant writes the storyboard: a direction block, the scenes and the shots, within Kleo’s limits. This is where the film’s quality is made.

05 · Credits first, then the render

Kleo checks everything and answers with the exact credits before it starts. Nothing is charged until the job is accepted. The render runs on a GPU rented for that job, typically 25–35 minutes, and the assistant reports progress.

06 · Download and publish

The assistant hands you a link to the finished MP4, good for 7 days. Review it, then upload it to your channel yourself: Kleo never publishes to YouTube or anywhere else.

Plan the duration and the credits together.

One credit covers two seconds of film, rounded up, with a minimum of 10 credits per render. A 20-second Short costs 10 credits; 60 seconds costs 30; 3 minutes costs 90; 5 minutes costs 150. The exact number is stated by Kleo before the render starts, and a refused request charges nothing.

Credits come in one-off packs, paid once through Stripe, in euro, with no subscription: €5 for 10 credits, €15 for 35, €40 for 100. Connecting gives you 7 credits with no form and no card; on their own they are below the 10-credit minimum, and with the €5 pack they make 17, enough for a 30-second Short at 15 credits. If you cancel a render, the unspent credits come back.

Shorter briefs are a good way to settle the tone before you spend 90 credits on a longer film.

What you receive.

  • FileOne MP4 in 4K, 3840×2160 for 16:9 or 2160×3840 for 9:16, at 60 fps.
  • LengthThe length you asked for, from 15 seconds to 5 minutes.
  • VoiceOne narrator, English or Italian, male or female.
  • PictureEvery shot generated as moving footage; no music, no captions, no on-screen text.
  • DeliveryA download link handed over by your assistant, good for 7 days.

Kleo’s film does not include music, captions or on-screen text. If your channel needs them, add them in your editor after the download. Uploading to YouTube remains your step: Kleo has no connection to your channel and never publishes for you. Review the film and your channel’s publishing requirements before you upload.
