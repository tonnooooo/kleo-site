Label illustrative reconstructions clearly

A generated reconstruction should not be presented as a recording of the event it illustrates. Decide how viewers will understand the status of the images through the narration, visible labels added in editing and the publication context.

A practical method

  1. Identify scenes depicting real events that were not actually filmed for this project.
  2. Choose clear wording that describes the images as illustrative.
  3. Review the destination platform's current disclosure requirements.

An example brief to adapt

Written illustration of this method; not a report of a rendered film or a customer result.

Plan an illustrative sequence accompanying a sourced historical account. Do not imply that the generated scenes are archival recordings. Add a production note identifying which scenes need an illustrative-reconstruction label in the final edit, and retain a source list for the factual narration.

Review before moving on

  • Could a viewer mistake the scene for evidence?
  • Is the label clear where it matters?
  • Does the description explain the production method?

Record a concrete observation beside each check: a line in the treatment, a source passage or a timestamp in the downloaded film. If you cannot inspect an item yet, leave it open. A request in a brief is an intention; the result is what must be reviewed.

What to avoid

A realistic appearance can strengthen an unintended claim of authenticity. Do not rely solely on a vague disclaimer placed far from the material it explains.
