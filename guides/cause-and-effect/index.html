Make scenes connect through cause and effect

A sequence becomes easier to follow when each event changes what can happen next. Replace a list of attractive images with decisions and consequences that the viewer can connect.

Kleo production guides · English · Editorial review 2026-09-23

A practical method

  1. Write because or therefore between consecutive scenes.
  2. Identify any transition that only means and then.
  3. Add the missing action or remove the disconnected scene.

An example brief to adapt

Written illustration of this method; not a report of a rendered film or a customer result.

Plan a 45-second animation about a gardener rescuing a dry seedling. The watering can is empty, so she collects rain in a cracked bowl; the bowl leaks, so she carries the seedling to the shelter instead. Keep the resolution connected to her decisions.

Review before moving on

  • Why does each scene happen now?
  • Can the viewer infer the connection?
  • Does narration explain rather than conceal missing action?

Record a concrete observation beside each check: a line in the treatment, a source passage or a timestamp in the downloaded film. If you cannot inspect an item yet, leave it open. A request in a brief is an intention; the result is what must be reviewed.

What to avoid

Do not use narration to claim that one event caused another when the visuals show no relationship. Revise the storyboard before adding more explanatory words.
