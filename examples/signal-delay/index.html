01The film

The film.

A documentary told as five months of mission logs. On November 14, 2023, Voyager 1 started repeating one pattern of ones and zeros, and every question cost forty-five hours of light-time. One narrator walks through the readout, the dead chip and the code moved by hand in 1970s assembly.

The opening 0:00–1:02 of Signal Delay, up to the title. Muted on the site and playing by itself while it is in view; the subtitles are part of the film.

What sits on the frame was decided by this film’s treatment: photographic stills with a slow push-in, hand-drawn amber diagrams, terminal windows, dates on black, two clocks in the corner, 35 mm grain, a 2.39:1 frame, a quiet music bed and subtitles. They are choices made for this documentary, not a standard part of every Kleo film. The standard film has one narrator and nothing else: no background music, no captions and no on-screen text.

03Make your own

Make your own.

Connect Kleo to the assistant you already use and ask for a narrated film: the subject, the length, the format and the look, realistic or animation. Kleo asks for whatever is missing, returns the treatment, then states the exact credits before the render starts; nothing is charged until the job is accepted. The finished MP4 arrives as a link that stays good for 7 days.

“Use Kleo to make a 2-minute narrated film about the day Voyager 1 started repeating one pattern of ones and zeros. Realistic look, English narration, 16:9. Show me the treatment and the credit cost before starting the render.”

A 2-minute film is 60 credits, and a film is made only for accounts that have bought a pack; a 60-second animatic of the same storyboard is 5 credits, for every account. The animation look, a 2D animated film on the same workflow, costs the same per second; its sample is the octopus Short.
